# EN: 25/9/2026 (Juan: «las dos») -- THE WAR MOVED THE PEOPLE OF THE BANDA ORIENTAL INTO MONTEVIDEO. Measured in Juan's
#     game and the headless runs of 25/9: Montevideo starts 1836 with 36,000 (~19% of the five Oriental states) and ends
#     1851-1852 with 23-26,000 (7-8%) while the countryside nearly doubles. The 1852 census counted the opposite:
#     Montevideo 33,994 of ~132,000 (~26%). The war emptied the countryside (levies, cattle driven off, families to
#     Brazil and Entre Rios, deaths) and filled the walled city with Europeans. Two state modifiers, on while the war
#     lasts (gg_war_demography_on) and taken off with the rest of the wartime ones (gg_wartime_modifiers_clear).
#     Calibration: a two-compartment model over the 1.13 pop-growth defines (birth 0.45%/month and mortality
#     0.35%/month at SoL 8; 0.40% and 0.21% at SoL 12), on the measured baseline, gives 21-25% in 1852 for SoL 5-8
#     in Montevideo and 10-12 in the countryside (16-18% if both are higher) from births and deaths alone, before the
#     1839-1842 internal migration adds to it (design repo, tools/modelo_poblacion_oriental.py). To be verified with
#     the GG_POP trace (gg_trace_oriental_population) in a headless run to 1853.
#     SOURCE / FUENTE: Censo General Administrativo de 1852 (Montevideo 33,994: 18,590 Orientals, 15,404 foreigners),
#     in Barrios Pintos, Montevideo: los barrios I (1971), p. 3; Uruguay ~132,000 in 1852 and 221,000 in 1860,
#     Montevideo 34,000 and 58,000: N. Duffau, «¿El infierno en Babel?» (2011); padron of Montevideo of October 1843
#     (Andres Lamas): 31,189, of whom 11,431 Orientals -- 5,324 French, 4,205 Italians, 3,406 Spaniards, 2,553
#     Argentines --, and more than 33,000 European immigrants landed 1835-1842, total population 128,371 in 1835:
#     M. Etchechury Barrera, «Defensores de la humanidad y la civilizacion», Historia 50/II (2017), n. 31-32; Oriental
#     State 180-200,000 in 1842, ~10,000 people moved off the Rio Negro districts by Rivera in January 1843, 1.7-2.2
#     million head of cattle sent to Rio Grande in 28 months, refugees of the countryside still in the city in November
#     1851: M. Etchechury Barrera, «Chinas, guayaquises y jente que no es de armas» (Redalyc 3801/380157595006);
#     A. Dumas, Montevideo ou une nouvelle Troie (Paris, 1850).

# EN: Montevideo while the Defensa (or, before the split, the Republic) holds it: the refugees of the countryside and
#     the European immigrants crowd behind the walls. Dumas called the besieged city «une nouvelle Troie» (1850).
#     Migration pull: the displaced of 1839-1843 come into the city while it shares a market with the countryside;
#     birth and mortality: the growth the immigrants brought after the split, when no one can walk in from the
#     countryside any more -- the sea lane stayed open and fed the plaza (gg_siege_the_sea_lane_stays_open).
# ES: Montevideo mientras la tiene la Defensa: los refugiados de la campaña y los inmigrantes europeos se amontonan
#     tras las murallas. Atraccion: los desplazados de 1839-1843; natalidad y mortalidad: el crecimiento que trajeron
#     los inmigrantes despues del corte, cuando ya nadie puede entrar caminando desde la campaña.
gg_war_montevideo_refuge = {
	icon = "gfx/interface/icons/timed_modifier_icons/modifier_flag_positive.dds"
	state_migration_pull_mult = 1.0
	# EN/ES: 25/9/2026 (AUD-MVD-POPS) -- 0.5/-0.5 -> 0.35/-0.35: with the immigrants arriving as pops
	#     (gg_montevideo_immigrants.txt) the model gives 32% at SoL 5; now 22-28% (tools/modelo_poblacion_oriental.py)
	#     / con los inmigrantes como pops el modelo daba 32%; ahora 22-28%
	state_birth_rate_mult = 0.35
	state_mortality_mult = -0.35
}

# EN: the other four Oriental states while the war lasts, whoever holds them: the levies take the men (lower births),
#     the armies and the slaughter kill (higher mortality), and the families leave for the city, Brazil and Entre Rios
