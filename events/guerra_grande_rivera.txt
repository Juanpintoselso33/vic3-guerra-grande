# EN: FRUCTUOSO RIVERA, 1842-1854. The man who founded the Partido Colorado, who threw Oribe out
#     in 1838 and whose government created the Defensa, spends the whole of the Guerra Grande
#     losing: Arroyo Grande (6 December 1842), India Muerta (27 March 1845), Cerro de las Animas
#     (January 1847). Between those defeats his own government strips him of his commands, bars
#     his return, watches the Empire jail him in Rio de Janeiro, tries to forbid him to land when
#     Brazil ships him home, and finally -- on 4 December 1847 -- has two of its own colonels
#     arrest him at Maldonado and deport him on a French vessel, "por todo el tiempo que dure la
#     presente guerra". He does not come back for the peace: Giro forbids it. He comes back in
#     November 1853, named to a triumvirate by the coup of a younger colorado, and dies on the
#     road to take office, on 13 January 1854, by the arroyo Conventos outside Melo.
#     The cleavage underneath is not blanco against colorado. It is caudillos against doctores,
#     and it runs horizontally through both parties (Pivel Devoto's reading, followed here). The
#     Defensa's own account of itself -- liberty, Europe, civilisation against caudillo barbarism
#     -- required the destruction of the caudillo who founded it.
# ES: FRUCTUOSO RIVERA, 1842-1854. El hombre que fundo el Partido Colorado, que volteo a Oribe en
#     1838 y cuyo gobierno creo la Defensa, se pasa la Guerra Grande entera perdiendo: Arroyo
#     Grande (6 de diciembre de 1842), India Muerta (27 de marzo de 1845), Cerro de las Animas
#     (enero de 1847). Entre esas derrotas su propio gobierno lo destituye de sus mandos, le
#     prohibe volver, mira como el Imperio lo encarcela en Rio de Janeiro, intenta prohibirle
#     desembarcar cuando el Brasil lo manda de vuelta y, finalmente -- el 4 de diciembre de 1847
#     --, hace que dos de sus propios coroneles lo detengan en Maldonado y lo deporten en un
#     buque frances, "por todo el tiempo que dure la presente guerra". No vuelve para la paz:
#     Giro se lo prohibe. Vuelve en noviembre de 1853, nombrado triunviro por el golpe de un
#     colorado mas joven, y muere yendo a asumir, el 13 de enero de 1854, junto al arroyo
#     Conventos, cerca de Melo.
#     El corte de abajo no es blancos contra colorados. Es caudillos contra doctores, y atraviesa
#     horizontalmente a los dos partidos (la lectura de Pivel Devoto, que es la que se sigue aca).
#     El relato que la Defensa hacia de si misma -- la libertad, Europa, la civilizacion contra la
#     barbarie caudillesca -- exigia destruir al caudillo que la habia fundado.
#
# Sources, with URLs and bilingual notes, in SOURCES_rivera.md.
#
# SCHEDULING. These events are scheduled from the end of each option of guerra_grande.3, inside
# the existing `c:URU ?= { ... }` block, with day offsets from a base of 10 February 1839 -- the
# same base the contingents and the loans use. Only gg_rivera.1 is scheduled from there; every
# other event in this file is triggered by the option the player picks in the one before it.
#
#   trigger_event = { id = gg_rivera.1 days = 1395 popup = yes }   # 1842.12.6  Arroyo Grande
#
# ROUTING. Every event here is addressed to the country holding guerra_grande_defensa_government,
# not to ROOT: a player who took the blanco road becomes CER via change_tag in guerra_grande.3,
# and in that game the Defensa is the AI. Scheduling by tag inside c:URU, and testing the
# variable in the trigger, means the Defensa gets this arc whether it is the player or the AI --
# the same fix gg_abolition.1 and the contingents already carry.

# EN: Roughly 8,000 a side on the Arroyo Grande, in Entre Rios near San Salvador. Oribe's right
#     under Urquiza and his brother Ignacio's cavalry break Rivera's centre; the beaten army
#     leaves 2,000 dead and 1,400 prisoners, the whole of its artillery and infantry, its
#     ammunition and 24,000 horses. The officers and, it is said, even the corporals and
#     sergeants are put to the knife; the rank and file are folded into Oribe's army. Federal
#     losses: 300. Rivera runs from the field leaving his sword and his pistols behind.
#     Oribe wastes weeks on minor business, which is the only reason the colorado government has
#     time to scrape 5,000 men together; Rosas's fleet ferries his infantry and guns across the
#     river, and on 16 February 1843 his vanguard camps on the Cerrito. He does not storm the
#     city. He sits down in front of it for eight and a half years.
# ES: Unos 8.000 por lado en el Arroyo Grande, en Entre Rios, cerca de San Salvador. La derecha
#     de Oribe con Urquiza y la caballeria de su hermano Ignacio rompen el centro de Rivera; el
#     ejercito batido deja 2.000 muertos y 1.400 prisioneros, toda su artilleria y su infanteria,
#     la municion y 24.000 caballos. A los oficiales y, se dice, hasta a los cabos y sargentos
#     los degüellan; la tropa se incorpora al ejercito de Oribe. Bajas federales: 300. Rivera
#     huye del campo dejando la espada y las pistolas.
#     Oribe pierde semanas en asuntos menores, que es la unica razon por la que el gobierno

			limit = { has_template = URU_Fructuoso_Rivera }

				}
			}

			name = gg_rivera_walls_stripped

#     finish Rivera. Rivera has a little over 4,000, mostly militia lately embodied; Urquiza has
#     3,000 veterans, and the last report before the battle puts him at 6,000 once the rearguard
#     under Francia and Basabilbaso comes up. Rivera calls a council of war at nine on the night
#     of the 26th and it votes to fight. Before dawn on the 27th a rider from Anacleto Medina
#     arrives with Medina's opinion: do not engage until the reinforcements come. Rivera engages.
#     Two hours. Between 400 and 1,000 dead on the field, 500 prisoners, the park, the horses,
#     his correspondence and his sword. Urquiza has the 500 prisoners' throats cut and, it is
#     said, hangs a paper from a mangrullo: "El que entierre uno de estos sera degollado."
#     Rivera crosses the Yaguaron at the Paso de las Piedras and asks Brazil for asylum.
#     The Defensa's answer is immediate and is the hinge of this whole arc: it strips him of his
#     military commands, gives the army to Anacleto Medina, and passes a rule forbidding his
#     return to Uruguay without the express authorisation of its own War Minister.
# ES: Rosas le manda a Oribe un cuerpo expedicionario de 4.000 entrerrianos al mando de Urquiza
#     con la orden de terminar con Rivera. Rivera tiene poco mas de 4.000, en buena parte milicia
#     recien incorporada; Urquiza tiene 3.000 veteranos, y el ultimo parte antes de la batalla lo
#     da en 6.000 cuando se le suma la retaguardia de Francia y Basabilbaso. Rivera reune consejo
#     de guerra a las nueve de la noche del 26 y el consejo vota dar batalla. Antes del amanecer
#     del 27 llega un ayudante de Anacleto Medina con la opinion de Medina: no comprometer un
#     encuentro hasta que lleguen los refuerzos. Rivera lo compromete.
#     Dos horas. Entre 400 y 1.000 muertos en el campo, 500 prisioneros, el parque, las caballadas,
#     su correspondencia y su espada. Urquiza manda degollar a los 500 prisioneros y, se dice,
#     cuelga un papel de un mangrullo: "El que entierre uno de estos sera degollado."

#     La respuesta de la Defensa es inmediata y es la bisagra de todo este arco: lo destituye de
#     sus cargos militares, le da el ejercito a Anacleto Medina y aprueba una disposicion que
#     prohibe su retorno al Uruguay salvo autorizacion expresa de su propio Ministro de Guerra.

		NOT = { has_global_variable = gg_rivera_india_muerta_settled }

			limit = { any_scope_character = { has_template = URU_anacleto_medina } }

				#     character_templates ever names (commander_rank_1/_2/_3). Rivera's arc fires
